Handleshare

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 29 September 2026

Handleshare is a digital business card that you fill in yourself and share on your own terms. Everything you put in the app stays on your device and, if you use iCloud, in your own private iCloud account, which we can't read. The one exception is a card link you choose to create, described below.

In short — No accounts. No tracking. No analytics. No advertising. Your cards never sit on a server of ours. Your card is shared only when you choose to share it, and a card link publishes only what you pick, until it expires.

What you put into Handleshare

Handleshare stores the information you choose to add to your card(s): your name, pronouns, title, company, phone, email, postal address, web and social links, booking and payment links, a short intro and bio, a profile photo, Wi-Fi network details (name, security type, and password), and any file attachments you add (for example a portfolio, menu, or resume).

All of this is stored locally on your device. If you're signed in to iCloud, it is also synced to your own private iCloud account (Apple's CloudKit private database, container iCloud.com.fbmore.Handleshare) so your cards appear across your devices and survive a reinstall. The developer of Handleshare has no access to this data. Only a card link you create (below) places anything in the app's shared database.

Sharing is always your call

Nothing leaves your device unless you initiate it. When you send your card itself — as a QR code, or as files through the iOS share sheet, email, or Messages — the information travels directly from your device to the person you're sharing with. There is no Handleshare server in between. QR codes (including Wi-Fi codes) are generated on your device.

The exception is a card link. One is made when you choose to share a link, and when the files you send by email or the share sheet add up to more than 20 MB, Handleshare sends them as a card link instead, telling you before it uploads. If you can't or don't want to make a link, you can send your card and files directly instead.

A note on Wi-Fi: your network name and password are part of your card data only because you typed them in, and they're shared only when you present the Wi-Fi QR code or explicitly include them in a share.

Card links

A card link publishes a contact card with the details you've chosen to share, plus any files you picked, to the app's own iCloud database — container iCloud.com.fbmore.Handleshare — at a random, unguessable web address. Anyone with the address can open the page, save the contact card and download any files. The records can't be listed or searched: only our link server can read them, one address at a time, and the page asks search engines not to index it. Apple stores the files; our link server runs on Cloudflare.

  • Only what you chose. The files you ticked, your note, and the card details you have switched on in the share sheet — which can include your name, title and company, photo, phone, email, links and postal address — shown on the page and offered as a contact card. Anything you switched off, and your other cards, stay private.
  • It expires. Free links last 7 days; Pro can pick 7, 30 or 90. When a link expires it stops opening right away, and the uploaded copies are deleted within a day. You can stop any link earlier from Plan & Settings → Card Links, which deletes them straight away.
  • View counts. The page pings a small counter we run on Cloudflare, which stores the random link id and a number, for up to 400 days. To keep storage in check and spot abuse, it also keeps daily totals per uploader, identified only by a short one-way hash of an iCloud record id — never your name, Apple ID or email. No IP addresses, no cookies, no profiles.
  • Downloads come straight from Apple's iCloud. When a browser blocks that direct fetch, the bytes pass through a relay we run on Cloudflare; it streams them through and keeps nothing.
  • Reporting. Every shared page carries a "Report this card" link that emails us the address so we can delete it.

Backups you create

Handleshare can save a backup file containing everything above — profiles, attachments, scans and photos — into its own folder on your iPhone, visible in Files under On My iPhone → Handleshare → Backups. It is created on your device and stays there unless you move or share it yourself. Anyone with access to that file can read its contents, so treat it like any other copy of your personal data: keep it somewhere you trust, and delete backups you no longer need.

Scanning other people's cards

If you scan a paper business card or a QR code, the resulting contact information is processed on your device and kept in a local scan history. If you choose to save a scanned contact, it is added to your device's Contacts — with your permission, using Apple's Contacts framework. Scanned data is not uploaded anywhere by Handleshare.

Device permissions

  • Camera — to scan QR codes and paper business cards.
  • Photo library — to pick a profile photo, and to save QR codes or card images if you export them.
  • Contacts — only to save contacts you scanned, when you ask it to.

Each permission is requested only when the feature needs it, and the app works without any of them.

Purchases

Handleshare Pro is sold through Apple's In-App Purchase. Apple handles the payment; we never see your card details. We use RevenueCat to check whether a purchase is active. RevenueCat receives an anonymous identifier for your install along with the purchase and subscription status tied to it — not your name, email, or anything from your cards. If you never buy Pro, nothing is sent.

Services your device contacts directly

Some features fetch things from the internet. These requests go from your device to the service named, never through us, and we receive nothing back:

  • OpenStreetMap / Nominatim — if your card includes a postal address and you export a card or PDF showing a map, that address is sent to Nominatim to look up its coordinates, and map tiles are fetched from OpenStreetMap.
  • unpkg.com — the map view loads its rendering library from this CDN, which sees your IP address.
  • Google's favicon service — when you scan a QR code containing a web address, the site's domain is sent to Google to fetch its icon for your scan history.
  • The sites you scan — to show a preview thumbnail in scan history, your device loads the scanned page, so that site sees your IP address as any visit would.

What we do not collect

  • No account, sign-in, or registration of any kind.
  • No analytics, usage tracking, crash reporting, or advertising SDKs.
  • No advertising identifier, and no tracking across other companies' apps or websites.
  • No location data.
  • No card content — names, numbers, photos, attachments, Wi-Fi credentials — is sent to the developer, except what you publish in a card link. Wi-Fi details are never part of a card link.

Third-party links

Your card can include links to external services (for example a Venmo, PayPal, or booking page). Tapping those links opens the external site or app; what happens there is governed by that service's own privacy policy. Handleshare never accesses those accounts.

Children's privacy

Handleshare is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect any personal information from them.
